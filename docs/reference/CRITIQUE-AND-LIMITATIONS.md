# Critical review of Symblic's contracts and usefulness

> **AI-written, revised.** The first version of this document was generated by an AI model (Gemini, through
> Google's Antigravity agent) in October 2026 and later corrected against the code by another AI agent. No
> human has reviewed it. See [../MADE-WITH-AI.md](../MADE-WITH-AI.md).

The central failure in the original pitch was the jump from **precise instructions** to **perfect model
execution**. Precision can narrow the intended task and make acceptance easier to check. It does not
make a probabilistic constructor infallible, guarantee that a proof will be found, or supply missing
facts about the world. Splitting a project into small tasks moves some difficulty into choosing the
boundaries, specifying their interfaces and composing their results.

The useful version of the idea is model-backed construction with explicit authority, independent
checks, bounded repair and retained evidence. It can support human formal statements as well as exact
computation and uncertain language work. Its value depends on whether this reduces total failure and
development cost for real tasks. Neither formal vocabulary nor a large adapter catalogue establishes
that result. The 2.5 reform is installed as live RC2, while its component and smoke evidence still do
not establish that empirical value. [REFORM-3](REFORM-3.md) maps all 27 critique families to remedies, checks and limitations;
[INTENSE-CRITIQUE-AND-LIMITATIONS](INTENSE-CRITIQUE-AND-LIMITATIONS.md) gives the wider conceptual review.

## 1. A checked contract can be the wrong contract

`assert` establishes the encoded predicate on the supplied values when that check executes successfully.
It does not establish that the predicate is the operator's complete intention. Several classic failures
are ordinary logical consequences of weak contracts:

```symblic
use profile=verified.
let passage = "The alloy contains copper and tin.".
let extracted: List of Text = [].
assert all item in extracted: contains(passage, item).
let summary = "Bananas are yellow.".
assert len(words(summary)) <= 12.
```

This program should pass. The first assertion constrains returned items without requiring any; the
second constrains length without constraining subject matter. Calling the result “grounded” would add
an unsupported conclusion. Adding `len(extracted) > 0` excludes a valid empty result in other tasks and
still would not require both metals. Completeness needs a declared universe, coverage obligations or
an independently justified semantic judgment.

The source-witness adapter now separates returned-item support from completeness relative to an
explicit finite catalog. Extractive summaries concatenate exact spans and cover caller-required span
IDs; free-form paraphrases remain proposals. Field extraction pins labels, aliases, source regions,
entity witnesses and declared surrounding literals. It rejects the wrong invoice number even when that
number occurs in an exact quote. These are useful bounded source relationships, not a parser for every
meaning of natural language. An incomplete or mistaken caller contract remains incomplete or mistaken.
[SOURCE-WITNESSES](SOURCE-WITNESSES.md)

The Interpreter now retains full compilation context and an exact-source requirement map. A check
expression found in the AST is labelled **present, not executed**, including when it occurs on an
unreachable branch. Unmapped hard requirements remain visible. Compiler-exposed examples are not
held-out validation. The unresolved problem is translating an informal requirement into an adequate
predicate; a model-generated assertion cannot certify that translation by endorsing itself.

## 2. Exact processing can preserve a false premise

A model may extract the wrong price and the kernel may multiply it perfectly. The resulting arithmetic
receipt establishes the multiplication, not the source association, the price's validity or the original
request's interpretation. Hashes bind content and versions; they do not make that content true or identify
an authenticated issuer by themselves. A source report can be false even when its quote is exact.

The live RC2 runner carries host-assigned origins, dependency references, scoped checks and support
conditions through derived bindings. A checked computation does not erase model-proposal or assumption
ancestry. The runner preserves contested vote status, propagates unavailable support, and detects
missing or mismatched references. Its generated claims start at revision 1 with no withdrawal, and its
assessment records have empty disagreement lists. Retry rollback removes rejected claims from the
active report registry while retaining the attempt trace.

The standalone `symblic.kernel.epistemic` evaluator separately supports supplied revisions, withdrawals and
explicit disagreement records, with downstream support checks. A separate persistent notebook now
connects those operations to imported local run snapshots and operator revisions in the installed 5 October reform. It retains
history, refuses unavailable selected support, clears prior checks on operator content revisions and makes hash-only
values visible. Its 32 Python checks and two desktop/phone browser journeys passed in isolation; it
was installed in the later 5 October reform, not the earlier RC2 release. Editing a notebook does not alter a completed run.
Evidence policy also remains separate from the selected logic: the proof checker is intuitionistic
propositional logic with elimination from
falsity, so it is **not** a paraconsistent calculus. Withholding contested premises from an action is a
policy decision, not a change to proof rules.

Exact rational arithmetic also does not make measured inputs exact. Explicit quantity records now
retain dimensions, intervals, tolerance declarations, provenance and validity. These establish
conditional enclosures under supplied measurement assumptions. They neither calibrate an instrument
nor determine whether a numerical model describes the phenomenon. [QUANTITIES](QUANTITIES.md)

## 3. Context isolation creates an information-selection problem

A fresh capsule can reduce irrelevant carryover and make dependencies inspectable. It does not erase
training priors, force the model to attend to every included statement, remove every injection pathway,
or guarantee a correct inference. Conversely, omitting relevant context need not force a hallucination:
a constructor can fail or refuse. The engineering question is whether required information was actually
available at the relevant step and how missing information is represented.

Verified runtime packing now distinguishes required selected values from optional context. It fails
before dispatch when required values plus fixed prompt overhead do not fit. The manifest identifies
included and omitted rows, hashes and reasons; repair text is counted before another call. Budget units
are Unicode characters, not model tokens. Ancestor lineage is retained as metadata without silently
restoring forgotten or unrequested parent raw values.

There is an earlier boundary in `.symd` papers: the compiler truncates a section above 8,000 characters
with a warning and selects earlier sections against a separate 10,000-character prose target. Definitions
and the current section are retained in that selection even if their combined size exceeds that target.
Runtime manifests describe the resulting bindings, not the original entire paper. Both compiler warnings
and runtime manifests matter. A future requirement-level omission map would improve this boundary;
“every ask read the whole paper” is currently false.

## 4. Repeatability has several different meanings

Pure computation, replay of recorded replies, fresh model generation and agreement across models are
different experiments. A compatible lock can reproduce recorded execution without a fresh provider call.
A formal checker can repeatedly evaluate a fixed candidate. Neither guarantees that another fresh model
call will construct that same candidate. Provider changes, input formatting and inference conditions must
be recorded or bounded before a fresh-run claim has a clear meaning.

Voting can improve performance on some distributions. It can also amplify a shared wrong convention.
Different provider names, unanimous votes or zero observed disagreement do not establish statistical
independence or correctness. Family lineage is a diversity filter, not a measurement of error correlation.
`strict` refusal handles an observed contested result; it cannot detect a unanimous error by itself.
Tightening now separates a candidate from adoption and preserves original requirements. Greater
agreement after a rewrite still may reflect a changed problem.

Earlier criticism invoked distributed-consensus impossibility as though it directly refuted output
comparison. That is too broad: comparing completed replies is not the same task as guaranteeing agreement
and termination in an asynchronous failure model. Symblic still needs explicit scheduling, timeout,
recovery and tie-breaking rules, but its behavior must be assessed against its actual operational model.

## 5. More formal capability needs a clear boundary

The criticism that “all useful work moved into the kernel” is valid when every new problem requires a
new bespoke solver and the model merely names its answer. The reform adds a different path: a human or
model constructs a proof term and a generic checker validates local inference rules against caller-held
premises and a goal. Finite quantified syntax lowers caller-declared domains to ground formulas. This
preserves model construction without asking the kernel to invent the derivation. Direct exact adapters
remain useful optional tools; adding the proof path does not require removing them.

The implemented interface and its injected-constructor tests establish this separation. They do not
establish successful real-model proof construction. The earlier nested-proof pilot accepted no proof;
47 of its 51 answered attempts failed before logical checking. Later flat and incremental formats
require their own model evaluation. [REFORM-3](REFORM-3.md#local-model-observations) records that pilot
separately from the new code/numerical task-card pilot.

The current fragments are bounded intuitionistic propositional proofs and explicit finite-domain
expansion, not unrestricted first-order logic, an automatic theorem prover or arbitrary prose semantics.
A resource refusal means the supplied work exceeded supported limits; it does not mean the proposition
is false, and it is not by itself an unsound inference. An accepted invalid derivation would be a
soundness bug. Tests provide implementation evidence; they do not prove the checker sound.
[PROOF-TERMS](PROOF-TERMS.md), [FINITE-LOGIC](FINITE-LOGIC.md)

Language design had additional concrete traps. Legacy unrecognized prose can invoke a model; explicit
and verified profiles reject that path. A bare false expression still displays false rather than failing
an obligation. Verified functions enforce transitive purity, while legacy keeps the routine bridge.
Verified modules isolate predicates under immutable source identities and explicit imports; legacy
shared predicates remain a compatibility mode. Strict coercion and visible transformation receipts
reduce silent loss, but choosing a weak type still creates a weak contract. These are operational
improvements, not evidence that a programming language can eliminate specification mistakes.

## 6. Composition and learning can multiply cost and failure

Small tasks are not automatically independent. Decomposition may discard global constraints, duplicate
work, introduce inconsistent assumptions or conceal an earlier failure behind later checked processing.
Retries, votes, stronger-model assistance, checker feedback and human debugging all cost resources. A
project assembled from locally passing units still needs integration checks against its intended outcome.
The existing code-generation, sandboxed JavaScript tests, spec sheets and Keep workflow remain useful;
finite fixtures constrain behavior only on the cases and assertions they actually exercise.

The agent-network direction is plausible when nodes have declared state, effects, budgets and objectives,
and edges have typed values and evidence-transfer rules. Feedback cycles need bounded schedules and
recovery behavior. Merely connecting API calls does not create an end-to-end differentiable neural
network or make an LLM literally a bagged decision tree. Related work already studies agent-graph
optimization and textual feedback, so novelty needs an explicit comparison and new evidence.
[GPTSwarm](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.16823), [TextGrad](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07496)

An earlier critique also wrongly treated discrete rewards as intrinsically untrainable. Expected-reward
gradients can be estimated without differentiating the checker itself. The actual risks are inadequate
rewards, weak or leaked tests, shared errors and deployment drift. The pure policy selector/evaluator has
isolated checks; durable execution bridges and learned routing require their own evidence. A candidate
selected on development outcomes does not become validated by reusing those outcomes as its test set.
[Williams, REINFORCE](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00992696)

Existing effect fences are another distinction the earlier critique missed: retries already restore
state, and effects/delegation are blocked across retry intervals, including transitive routine paths.
Ledgered recovery refuses an uncertain non-idempotent redispatch. Those mechanisms must be preserved and
tested as graph paths expand; their existence does not prove every new composition is safe.

## 7. Usefulness must be demonstrated against realistic alternatives

Symblic can be useful where a candidate is difficult to construct but useful acceptance checks are
available: bounded proofs, source-witnessed extraction, small program components, exact downstream
computation, controlled experiments and auditable automation. The acceptance contract must be cheaper
or more reusable than manually implementing each answer, and refusal must be an acceptable outcome.
For a straightforward deterministic calculation, an ordinary program may be simpler. For open-ended
interpretation, the system can organize uncertainty but cannot replace independent judgment with a type.

The next empirical comparison must hold information and budgets constant across direct prompting, typed
output, ordinary tool use, retries, witness construction and graph composition. Count every planned
case, including refusal, timeout, missing outcomes and stronger-model fallback. Keep checker-visible
fixtures separate from frozen hidden outcomes and report false acceptance, task success, cost, latency
and human specification/debugging effort. The matched evaluation implementation now has isolated checks;
that is not a real-model superiority result. The subsequent frozen six-cell task-card pilot completed
with zero accepted projects, five check failures and one deadline stop. Some code units passed exposed
checks, but no final artifact existed for held-out or final equality assessment. This is exploratory
feasibility evidence under those prompts and limits, not a matched comparison or general impossibility
result. [MATCHED-EVALUATION](MATCHED-EVALUATION.md),
[pilot scope](REFORM-3.md#local-model-observations)

Unsupported claims about universal determinism, automatic truth grounding, model-family independence,
lossless logarithmic context, physics-derived hallucination rates or polynomial-time general optimization
should be removed or turned into falsifiable hypotheses. The mathematical adapters retain their declared
finite domains and research uses. The ambition of an epistemic operating system becomes concrete only as
source, intent, checks, disagreement, revision, effects and recovery share operational contracts that users
can inspect. Broader logical fragments, semantic fidelity, learned topology, migration tooling and strong
empirical comparisons remain substantial work. [RESEARCH-ADOPTION](RESEARCH-ADOPTION.md)
